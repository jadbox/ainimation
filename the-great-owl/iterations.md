# The Great Owl Generation Iterations

- Iterations run: 2
- Final result: accepted

## Iteration 1 (rejected)
- Reviewer score: 56/100
- Learned: Requesting a 'style guide with detail callouts' makes the generator invent titles and text labels; callouts must be specified as wordless magnified insets joined by leader lines, and the no-typography rule must be stated positively in the layout description, not just in the avoid list.
- Feedback: STRENGTHS: The owl itself is close to target — mottled brown/gray plumage, enormous glowing amber eyes with highlights, hooked ivory beak, ear tufts, sagging cheek feathers, hunched monumental perch, and consistent front/profile/back turnarounds. Hero staging delivers the hollow-tree night scene: cold moonlight shaft, teal bioluminescent glow, indigo/slate shadows, icy-cyan rim light, dust motes, and soft-focus bark framing the edges. Linework reads dark brown and hand-inked with decent feather texture. HARD FAILURES: (1) The sheet is covered in typography — a 'THE GREAT OWL' title, FRONT/PROFILE/BACK VIEW labels duplicated above AND below the turnarounds, DETAIL CALLOUTS, EYE, TALON, FEATHER TEXTURE, SILHOUETTE READ — directly violating the zero text/labels mandate. (2) A misspelled label 'BEAK & CHUK FEATHERS' (should be CHEEK) would be an embarrassing production error. (3) The duplicated view labels are redundant layout noise. SECONDARY ISSUES: the right panel's clean cream digital style-guide layout and the smooth airbrushed gradient in the moonlight shaft push toward modern digital illustration rather than a 1980s hand-painted gouache model sheet; brush grain should be visible across every surface including the background light shaft. No anatomy glitches or extra limbs detected; talon count and eye construction read correctly.
- Prompt change: Replaced with the reviewer's complete revised prompt.

### Prompt Used for Image Generation
```
Create a polished The Great Owl character style guide for a hand-painted,
early-1980s dark fantasy animated feature. Deliver one cohesive 16:9 reference
image with a large three-quarter hero pose plus smaller front, profile, and back
turnaround views, detail callouts for key features, and a clear silhouette read.

The Great Owl is a feared, ancient owl whose counsel sends Mrs. Frisby to the rats: a massive ancient owl with mottled brown and gray feathers, enormous glowing amber eyes with stacked white highlights, a hooked ivory beak, feathered ear tufts, taloned feet, a hunched monumental perched silhouette with sagging cheek feathers. Give the
character no garment; the animal body is fully visible with tufted spiky feathers breaking the silhouette. Keep the animal anatomy visible beneath it.

Use hand-inked tapered dark-brown linework, two-tone cel shading, dense gouache
and watercolor texture on paper, and deliberately irregular fur/feather
silhouettes. Stage the hero pose at rodent eye level inside a hollow tree at night: one cold moonlight shaft plus faint bioluminescent teal glow are the practical sources, with deep indigo and slate-purple shadows, a continuous icy-cyan rim light, floating dust motes, and soft focus bark and roots occluding roughly one quarter of the frame edges. The pose should feel
ancient, feared, and wise, reading as a real animal first and a sage second. Avoid clean vector lines, smooth plastic rendering, modern clothing, flat lighting, extra limbs, duplicate features, text, watermarks, and logos.
```

## Iteration 2 (accepted)
- Reviewer score: 90/100
- Learned: Stating the no-typography rule positively in the layout description and specifying wordless circular insets joined by leader lines completely eliminated text; the remaining refinement target is pushing visible brush grain into the light effects themselves, since generators still default to smooth digital glow inside shafts and bioluminescent washes.
- Feedback: STRENGTHS: All prior hard failures are resolved — the sheet is 100% free of text, labels, titles, and watermarks, and the misspelled-caption risk is gone. The wordless callout system works exactly as intended: circular magnified insets for the amber eye (with stacked white highlights), hooked ivory beak with sagging cheek feathers, zygodactyl talon, and mottled feather texture, all tied to the figures by thin hand-drawn leader lines and arrows. The solid-black silhouette study and unlabeled hand-painted swatch chips read as authentic 1980s model-sheet furniture. Character identity is on-model across all four views: massive hunched owl, mottled brown/gray plumage with spiky silhouette-breaking tufts, enormous glowing amber eyes, ear tufts, sagging cheek feathers, no garment, true animal anatomy with zero glitches, zero extra limbs, and consistent turnaround construction. The hero staging hits the brief: rodent eye level in the hollow tree, cold moonlight shaft, teal bioluminescent glow, deep indigo/slate-purple shadows, a continuous icy-cyan rim light tracing the full silhouette, floating dust motes, and soft-focus bark/roots occluding roughly a quarter of the frame edges. Linework is tapered dark brown, cel shading is two-tone, and the aged warm paper unifies hero panel and turnaround panel as one hand-painted sheet — the rough gouache border on the hero panel is a genuinely period-correct touch. RESIDUAL MINOR ISSUES (not disqualifying): the moonlight shaft and the teal glow halo behind the hero still render smoother than the surrounding paint — a Bluth-era background would show dry-brush grain inside the light itself; the turnaround figures carry slightly less paint texture than the hero; and the silhouette study sits a touch small relative to the sheet. These are polish notes, not production blockers.
- Prompt change: None; the reviewer accepted this image.

### Prompt Used for Image Generation
```
Create a polished The Great Owl character reference sheet for a hand-painted, early-1980s dark fantasy animated feature in the Don Bluth tradition. Deliver one cohesive 16:9 image: a large three-quarter hero pose on the left, and on the right smaller front, profile, and back turnaround views, plus magnified circular detail insets of the eye, the beak with sagging cheek feathers, the talon, and the feather texture, each inset connected to the figure by thin hand-drawn leader lines and arrows, plus one solid-black silhouette study and one small row of unlabeled color swatch chips. Absolutely no text of any kind anywhere in the image: no title, no labels, no captions, no letters, no numbers, no watermarks, no logos — all information must be communicated visually only through the drawings, leader lines, and swatches.

The Great Owl is a feared, ancient owl whose counsel sends Mrs. Frisby to the rats: a massive ancient owl with mottled brown and gray feathers, enormous glowing amber eyes with stacked white highlights, a hooked ivory beak, feathered ear tufts, taloned feet, a hunched monumental perched silhouette with sagging cheek feathers. No garment; the animal body is fully visible with tufted spiky feathers breaking the silhouette. True owl anatomy throughout: one head, two eyes, two wings, zygodactyl talons, no extra or duplicated features, and all turnaround views consistent with the hero pose.

Render with hand-inked tapered dark-brown linework (never black vector lines), rich two-tone cel shading, and dense gouache and watercolor texture on aged warm paper with visible brush grain on every surface. Avoid smooth digital gradients, airbrushed glow, and plastic shading — even the light shaft and background must show painted texture. Deliberately irregular feather silhouettes.

Stage the hero pose at rodent eye level inside a hollow tree at night: one cold moonlight shaft plus a faint bioluminescent teal glow as the practical light sources, deep indigo and slate-purple shadows, a continuous icy-cyan rim light tracing the owl's silhouette, floating dust motes, and soft-focus bark and roots occluding roughly one quarter of the frame edges. The pose should feel ancient, feared, and wise — a real animal first and a sage second. Keep the turnaround panel on the same aged paper so the entire sheet reads as one hand-painted 1980s production model sheet, not a modern digital style guide. Avoid clean vector lines, smooth plastic rendering, modern clothing, flat lighting, extra limbs, duplicate features, and any typography whatsoever.
```
