# Movie & Style Specifications — The Rats of NIMH

This document contains the artistic, narrative, character, lighting, and animation specifications for **The Rats of NIMH** short film project. The automated AI animation pipeline reads configuration settings from `movie.yml` (and `movie.md`) to guide generation and review loops across character style sheets, background plates, scene scripts, and animated clips.

---

## 1. Core Feeling & Emotional Register
* **Cozy Danger**: Warmth and safety exist, but they are small pockets of light inside overwhelming darkness. Every interior feels like a candle-lit den surrounded by threat.
* **Reverence & Awe**: Magic is treated as ancient, heavy, and sacred—objects glow as if they have inner life (the Red Amulet, the lantern).
* **Vulnerability at Small Scale**: Characters are tiny creatures in a world built of giant human refuse. The mood is tender, hushed, and slightly mournful.
* **Storybook Gothic**: Ornate, curved, organic shapes; nothing is modern or geometric. Beatrix Potter crossed with gothic illustration.

## 2. Character Design & Specifications

### Main Characters in *Mrs Frisby and the Rats of NIMH*
* **Mrs Frisby**: A widowed field mouse and the story's determined protagonist, seeking a way to save her sick son Timothy.
* **Timothy NameFrisby**: Mrs. Frisby's young son, whose pneumonia prevents the family from leaving their home before plowing season.
* **Mr Ages**: An elderly, reclusive mouse physician and herbalist who treats Timothy and directs Mrs. Frisby to the rats.
* **Nicodemus**: The wise, aged leader of the rats of NIMH and keeper of their history and plans.
* **Justin**: The capable, compassionate captain of the rats' guard who helps Mrs. Frisby.
* **Jenner**: An ambitious rat who opposes Nicodemus's leadership and the plan to leave the farm.
* **Dragon**: The Fitzgibbons' formidable house cat, whose patrols make the rats' work dangerous.
* **The Great Owl**: A feared, ancient owl whose counsel sends Mrs. Frisby to the rats.

### Proportions & Anatomy
* **Scale**: Anthropomorphic rodents standing 2–2.5 heads tall, with realistic animal anatomy underneath cartoon acting.
* **Structure**: Large heads, pear-shaped bodies, digitigrade legs, small rounded feet, and long expressive tails that drag, curl, and balance poses.
* **Silhouette & Line of Action**: Poses built around dynamic C-curves and S-curves. Every frame must pass the "silhouette test" (reading instantly as a solid black shape).
* **Ears**: Oversized relative to the head—a key expression/cuteness lever. Ear interiors are saturated salmon-pink with subtle inner rim shading.
* **Posture**: Read as real animals first, people second—hunching, sniffing, crouching, and holding hands up like paws when anxious.

### Faces & Eyes
* **Eyes**: The entire performance center. Enormous, glossy, deep-set, featuring:
  * Large irises with visible radial texture.
  * 2–3 stacked white specular highlights.
  * Thick, dark upper lash line with minimal lower lid.
* **Muzzles & Mouths**: Short muzzles with small wet pink/red noses (bright highlight dot on the nose tip). Mouths feature expressive upper-lip flex; small sharp white incisors and pink tongues are exposed during emotional peaks.
* **Aging Indicators**: Heavy lids, sagging cheek fur, drooping whiskers/mustaches, and milky or dimmed eyes (elders).

### Fur & Hands
* **Fur Mechanics**: Rendered as spiky, spiky tufts breaking the silhouette—swept head tufts, cheek ruffs, chest ruffs. Never smooth contours.
* **Hands**: Small, four-fingered, pink or pale gray, dexterous and humanlike in gesture (clasped, trembling, clutching fabric). Elders feature gnarled, bony hands with long claw-nails and visible knuckles.

### Clothing & Fabrics
* **Minimalist Garments**: One signature garment per character (never full outfits). Animal bodies stay visible; clothing acts as a rustic accessory (capes, shawls, robes, hoods).
* **Weathered Edges**: Frayed, zigzag hems—edges are ragged, never clean.
* **Drapery**: Drawn with weight and economy: few, large triangular folds; fabric hangs and bunches heavily, reacting to momentum.
* **Costume Palette**: Muted solids that double as character identifiers against dark backgrounds.

### Signature Character Recipes
* **Mrs. Brisby**: Dusty mauve-brown fur, cream muzzle/chest, blue-violet eyes, pink ears/nose, anxious upright posture, hands held near chest. *Signature garment*: tattered red hooded cape.
* **Mr. Ages**: White-gray fur, hunched posture, round spectacles, mustache, wrapped in a drab gray shawl—a hermit alchemist silhouette.
* **Nicodemus**: Ghostly pale-gray fur, enormous flowing white beard and brows, gnarled hands, deep teal robe with purple oval motifs, one milky blind eye, hunched monumental presence.

## 3. Character Movement Guide (Animation Mechanics)

### Frame Rate & Fluidity
* **Animating on Ones (24 fps)**: Used for intense action, fast turns, magical transformations, physical impacts, water/fire FX, and key emotional breakdowns to provide ultra-smooth, feature-quality weight.
* **Animating on Twos (12 fps)**: Standard baseline for dialogue and moderate movement.
* **Dynamic Frame Ramping**: Seamless transitions between 2s for setup and 1s for action climaxes.

### Principles of Motion
* **Squash & Stretch Ratios**: Exaggerated elastic compression on impact or jump preparation, immediately balanced by realistic physical mass so characters feel fleshy, not rubbery.
* **Anticipation & Recoil**: Deep low-crouch anticipation poses before major actions (jumping, fleeing). Heavy recoil on landing with secondary body bounce in bellies, cheeks, and ears.
* **Drag & Overlapping Action**: Secondary elements never stop simultaneously with the torso:
  * Ear tips, whisker ends, tail tips, and tattered capes trail behind primary motion (*drag*).
  * On body stop, tail and clothing swing forward past the stopping point before settling (*follow-through*).
* **Held Emotional Poses ("Holding the Beat")**: Characters freeze primary body poses during key dramatic moments while micro-movements stay active:
  * Trembling fingers clutching fabric.
  * Rapid double-blinks and widening irises.
  * Heavy chest breathing and subtle whisker twitches.

## 4. Background, Foreground & Environments

### Background Art (Mediums & Texture)
* **Traditional Mediums**: Dense opaque gouache, transparent watercolor washes, and cel-vinyl/acrylic glazes painted on textured, heavy-tooth paper (e.g., Arches/Whatman grain).
* **Textural Techniques**: Heavy sponge-dabbing, dry-brushing for moss/bark, salt/alcohol washes for rock faces, and fine splatter for dirt, damp mist, and ground debris.
* **Color Temperature Transitions**: Backgrounds shift from warm amber/gold at light epicenters to cool indigo/slate purple in ambient shadow zones.
* **Architecture & Nature**: Gothic organic forms—gnarled tangled roots, arched stone burrows, sagging wooden beams, rusted iron grates. Zero straight edges or digital symmetry.

### Foreground Framing & Macro Depth
* **Framing Silhouettes**: Dark, out-of-focus foreground elements (vines, roots, cracked glass, overgrown thorn bushes) frame 15–30% of the screen borders, enhancing the "peeping through shadows" feel.
* **Macro Scale Representation**: Human objects loom gargantuan—a single rusted sewing needle looks like a broadsword; a tractor tire appears as a towering monolith; a glass canning jar acts as a room.
* **Foreground Blur**: Soft optical lens defocus applied to extreme foreground elements to simulate physical macro lens shallow depth-of-field.

## 5. Scene Directing & Lighting Manual

### A. Dramatic Staging & Spatial Composition
* **Rodent Macro Camera Placement**: The virtual lens stays fixed between 2 to 12 inches above ground level. Characters never feel "filmed from above" unless the shot deliberately represents a predator's point-of-view.
* **Looming Vertical Ratio**: Environments dominate 70% of frame volume; characters occupy the lower third to visually reinforce physical vulnerability.
* **Gothic Arch Framing & Triangulation**: Frame characters within natural environmental arches—curving tree roots, bent stalks, arched stone tunnels, or rusted horseshoe openings—to visually trap or shelter them.
* **Asymmetrical Rule of Thirds**: Place main characters off-center, balanced on the opposite third by a massive environmental mass (e.g., a towering glass jug or a Great Owl's claw).
* **Voyeuristic Depth Occlusion**: Always obscure 20–30% of foreground frame edges with dark, soft-focus silhouettes (hanging cobwebs, roots, cracked glass edges) to create an intimate, "peeping into a secret world" perspective.

### B. Technical Lighting Schemas & Chiaroscuro
* **Single Practical Point-Source Dominance**: Every shot orbits a single identifiable physical light source (a flickering candle, glowing Red Amulet, spark plug arc, or a shaft of sunlight through dirt cracks).
* **Chiaroscuro Value Distribution**:
  * *High-Contrast Darkness*: 60–70% of frame area in deep shadow or low-value tones.
  * *Ambient Fill*: 15–20% in cool, muted midtones.
  * *Intense Light Core*: 10–15% in saturated, radiant highlights.
* **Warm vs. Cool Temperature Dichotomy**:
  * Key lights use warm temperatures (2000K–3200K: amber, gold, crimson).
  * Ambient fill shadows push cool temperatures (7000K–10000K: teal, indigo, slate purple).
  * *Example*: In Mr. Ages' lab, warm amber lantern light strikes the front of characters, backed by deep teal/indigo shadows in the background jars.
* **360-Degree Rim Lighting**: Characters are encircled with bright rim lighting (backlighting) in gold or icy cyan, detaching dark fur silhouettes from dark gouache backgrounds.
* **Volumetric Air & Airbrush Light Washes**: Light beams must illuminate suspended environmental motes (floating dust, steam, rain droplets). Background plates feature soft airbrushed light washes radiating from light sources to emulate lens scattering and bloom.

### C. Color Scripting & Emotional Palette Mapping
* **Sanctuary / Safety**:
  * *Palette*: Warm ambers, rich ochres, terracotta, soft candlelight golds, deep umber shadows.
  * *Usage*: Brisby cinderblock home, interior mouse burrows.
* **Ancient Alchemy & Intellectual Mystery**:
  * *Palette*: Bioluminescent teal, emerald green, deep cobalt blue, glowing purple accents.
  * *Usage*: Nicodemus's study, NIMH lab flashback, Fitzgibbons' farm at night.
* **Immediate Danger / Mortality**:
  * *Palette*: Blood crimson, fiery orange, deep magenta, pitch black void shadows.
  * *Usage*: Great Owl's hollow tree, Dragon the Cat stalk shots, moving tractor blade encounters.
* **Sacred / Supernatural Magic**:
  * *Palette*: Pure radiant scarlet red, intense golden-white core, blue-cyan energy arcing.
  * *Usage*: The Red Amulet, stone levitation sequence.

### D. Camera Directing, Pacing & Cutting Mechanics
* **Slow Establishing Push-In**: Open scenes with 4 to 8 second slow multiplane push-ins, descending from intimidating landscapes (the thorn bush, hollow oak) down into the candlelit sanctuary.
* **Lateral Tracking with Parallax Acceleration**: On character movement, track horizontally with fast-moving foreground elements whipping past while deep background levels drift slowly.
* **Contrast Cuts**: Cut between pitch-black wide shots and sudden blinding light/impact shots (e.g., spark plug flash, owl eyes snapping open) to jar the audience.
* **Held Emotional Beats & Environmental Micro-Motion**: During emotional dialogues, hold the primary character pose completely still ("hold the beat") while secondary elements remain active (flickering candle flame, swaying hanging roots, rising steam).

### E. Optical Special Effects & Multi-Pass Exposure Directing
* **Backlit Emissive Exposure Passes**: Isolate glowing elements (eyes, amulet, sparks, magic portals) onto separate high-contrast black-and-white light mattes for optical pass processing.
* **Double-Exposure Bloom & Diffusion Bleed**: Apply soft diffusion bloom (20% Pro-Mist equivalent) to backlit passes so light physically bleeds across inked cel outlines onto surrounding dark background gouache.
* **Multi-Point Specular Star Highlights**: Direct 4-point star specular glints onto glass jar rims, metal brass fixtures, liquid droplets, and eyes during emotional climaxes.

## 6. Camera Behavior & Cinematography

* **Low Macro Camera Angles**: The camera rests at or below rodent eye-level, tilting upward to emphasize environmental scale and threat.
* **Multiplane Camera Parallax**: Backgrounds split into 4–5 distinct camera glass levels moving at differential speeds:
  1. *Level 1 (Foreground)*: Out-of-focus dark roots/vines moving fastest across frame.
  2. *Level 2 (Cel Layer)*: Characters and interactable props.
  3. *Level 3 (Midground)*: Furniture, workbench clutter, interior walls moving at standard tracking speed.
  4. *Level 4 (Background)*: Deep room corners, cavern walls, distant roots moving slowly.
  5. *Level 5 (Extreme Background)*: Distant glowing windows or deep shadows moving at minimal speed.
* **Dynamic Tracking & Sweeps**: Slow, dramatic crane shots that start low in grass/roots and rise up to reveal sprawling subterranean sets, or sweeping tracking shots following running characters.
* **Impact Camera Shake**: Physical multi-axis camera shake applied on heavy impacts (e.g., Great Owl landing, tractor engine rumbling, collapsing dirt).
* **Lens Artifacts**: Optical bloom around glowing light sources, subtle chromatic aberration at lens edges, and continuous floating dust-mote particles lit by volumetric light shafts.

## 7. Style Checklist (Do / Don't)

| Category | Do | Don't |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Line Art** | Tapered ink lines, soft dark brown/colored outlines, sketchy energy | Vector-clean outlines, pure black hard outlines |
| **Characters** | Tufted spiky fur, giant glossy eyes with 2–3 highlights, 1 ragged garment, dynamic C/S-curves | Smooth untextured fur, full modern outfits, clean hems, rigid stiff poses |
| **Lighting & Staging** | Single practical source, warm vs cool chiaroscuro, 360° rim lighting, 20-30% foreground occlusion | Uniform overall lighting, flat shadows, lack of depth occlusion, center-only lighting |
| **Movement** | Animating on ones for action, follow-through drag on ears/tails, held emotional poses with micro-blinks | Static robotic movement, uniform linear movement, lack of anticipation/recoil |
| **Camera & Optics** | Low macro angles, multiplane parallax (4–5 layers), foreground blur, impact camera shake | High flat camera angles, single-plane flat scrolling, digital sharpness |
| **Environments** | Cluttered found-object sets, deep vignettes, gouache texture, organic curved shapes | Modern props, clean symmetrical rooms, plastic smooth finishes |
